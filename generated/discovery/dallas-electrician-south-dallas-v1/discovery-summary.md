# Discovery Summary

## 1. Business Snapshot

Red Brick Home Electric is a small Dallas residential electrical contractor focused on older-home repair and service work across South Dallas and Oak Cliff. The business already saves enough permit, callback, and photo evidence to test whether repeated correction paths can become a local operational moat.

## 2. Likely Moat Candidates

- Older-home service and grounding correction memory tied to Dallas inspection outcomes
- Neighborhood-specific readiness patterns for South Dallas and Oak Cliff housing stock
- A reusable reinspection playbook built from reviewed failed-to-passed sequences

## 3. Best First Eval To Run

Recommended next action is the strongest first eval because the business already describes repeated callback loops where the exact correction path matters more than generic advice.

## 4. Most Important Missing Data

- Reviewed mappings from terse failed inspection notes to normalized failure labels
- A compact sample of correction steps that preceded successful reinspections
- Structured tags for older-home conditions discovered after the estimate

## 5. Recommendation For The Next 1 To 2 Weeks

1. Review 20 to 40 older-home failed inspections and record the correction path that led to the next pass.
2. Normalize the top repeated grounding, service-equipment, and documentation failure labels into the Dallas eval vocabulary.
3. Compare pattern slices by ZIP code and job type to see whether older-home locality effects are strong enough to influence estimating and readiness.
