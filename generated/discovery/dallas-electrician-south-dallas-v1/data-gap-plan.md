# Data Gap Plan

## Priority Order

### 1. Failed Inspection And Callback Notes

- Source name: inspector result notes plus internal callback notes
- Why it matters: provides the clearest label source for repeated older-home correction patterns
- Availability: partly exists internally
- Collection difficulty: medium
- Privacy sensitivity: medium
- Expected lift for eval quality: high
- Recommended collection order: first

### 2. Reinspection Outcome Sequences

- Source name: permit timelines with failed and subsequent passed inspection rows
- Why it matters: anchors recommended-next-action and approval-path supervision
- Availability: partly exists publicly and internally
- Collection difficulty: medium
- Privacy sensitivity: low
- Expected lift for eval quality: high
- Recommended collection order: second

### 3. Older-Home Photo Evidence

- Source name: service repair and grounding photos attached to callback jobs
- Why it matters: helps explain what changed between failure and pass for ambiguous notes
- Availability: exists internally but fragmented
- Collection difficulty: high
- Privacy sensitivity: medium
- Expected lift for eval quality: medium
- Recommended collection order: third

### 4. Dallas Electrical Guidance Snippets

- Source name: public inspection guidance, permit reminders, and code sheets
- Why it matters: improves explanation quality and supports retrieval-backed recommendations
- Availability: public
- Collection difficulty: low
- Privacy sensitivity: low
- Expected lift for eval quality: medium
- Recommended collection order: fourth

## Immediate Recommendation

Start with a reviewed set of 20 to 40 older-home residential failures where the team can reconstruct:

- permit subtype
- failed inspection note
- older-home condition discovered onsite
- actual correction step taken
- whether the next inspection passed

That sample is enough to test whether older-home correction memory can be turned into a reusable Dallas next-action artifact before broader ingestion work.
